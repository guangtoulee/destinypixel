## What the reader gets

The existing article already close-reads the image, records Waite, reads upright and reversed proportion, compares nearby cards, and keeps both cases, the practice and the misreading note. It did not give Rachel Pollack’s etymology of Temperance, her reading of the misaligned cups, why Temperance sits between Death and the Devil/Tower, or her reversed reading of extremes. Those three points are now in the Chinese article once each.

1. After the last paragraph of「手上在调水，脚下跨着两处」, with no new heading: Latin *temperare* means mixing in proportion, not moral abstinence; the cups are not vertically aligned and the water still enters the lower cup, which she calls a physically impossible arrangement; one foot in water and one on shore are unfinished possibility and the concrete world. Pages 139–141. The pillars-become-mountains clause is omitted. Marked as her symbolic reading, not Waite’s text.
2. After「把相近的牌义分清楚」, a new subsection titled exactly「为什么节制夹在死神与恶魔、高塔之间」(`depth-sequence`): a short Iris / iris-flower line for calm after the storm that follows Death; Temperance is the balance after Death that the Devil and the Tower will disturb, with links to those pages’ matching anchors. Page 141.
3. After the reversed section「逆位 找出不协调的具体位置」, with no new heading: reversed Temperance can be extremes, a life broken into fragments, and not yet knowing which action fits. Evidence is required. It does not default to “more imbalance.” Page 143. The return-to-Strength sentence is omitted.

No BOTA lion-eagle, no Wirth, no long Styx myth, no tetragrammaton, no long Chariot essay, and no new case was added. Page 142 is part of the chapter receipt and is not cited in the body.
