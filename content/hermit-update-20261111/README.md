## What the reader gets

The existing article already close-reads the image, records Waite, reads upright and reversed solitude, compares nearby cards, and keeps both cases, the practice and the misreading note. It did not give Rachel Pollack’s reading of the lantern, why the Hermit steps back before the Wheel, or her short upright and reversed limits. Those three points are now in the Chinese article once each.

1. After the last paragraph of「灯提在外面，人站在高处」, with no new heading and in one paragraph: Pollack reads the hexagram in the lantern as pointing both inward and outward. Hermit chapter page 106. In the Rider-Waite version the light is visible but still inside the lantern; it is released only through a conscious process open to anyone. Page 109. The long staff is conscious support, contrasted with the Fool waving a stick more by instinct. Page 109. A therapist and dreams are examples of a possible guide in her text, not advice to start or stop therapy and not a dream diagnosis. This does not mean an older person is more reliable, and it does not require seclusion or taking a teacher.
2. After「把相近的牌义分清楚」, a new subsection titled exactly「为什么隐士要先退后，下一张才是命运之轮」(`depth-sequence`): after Strength, and before the Wheel turns to its second half, first step back. The lonely-peak desolation is only part of the truth. Hermit chapter page 108. The Wheel chapter is cited only as「退后看见整轮」. Wheel chapter page 115. Stepping back does not require seclusion and does not override Waite. Strength links to its existing page only, because that page has no `depth-sequence` anchor. The Wheel links to the existing `depth-sequence` anchor.
3. Two short insertions, with no new headings. After the upright paragraphs: withdrawal is first a shift of inner attention; one need not leave the situation or go into seclusion. Hermit chapter page 110. A mentor is more often someone nearby who is already helping, or you yourself when you are helping someone else. Pages 110–111. Good guidance still does not require handing over one’s judgment. After the reversed paragraphs: a short note on「抽离走样」. It may be fear of others and growing isolation, or this moment may simply need interaction with people. Page 111. Her Peter Pan remark is an author metaphor for avoiding responsibility for one’s own life, not a diagnosis or a label. Needing a boundary, rest, or temporarily not wanting to socialize is not the same as escape, and the card does not force socializing. The existing solitude-effect checks and the rule that Waite’s concealment wording is not used to accuse anyone of deception stay verbatim. Pollack supplements those layers. The card does not replace psychological support or medical judgment.
