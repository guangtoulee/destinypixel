# Ten Cards, Too Many Stories? Give Every Celtic Cross Position a Job

A Celtic Cross can feel like ten guests with their microphones on. Each card has something to say, yet the answer becomes harder to hear. Before drawing more cards, give every position a clear question.

> Confirm the position definitions, connect the cards in groups, then write one provisional answer you can check against real information.

## Read positions and images together

In Chapter 11 of Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom, Rachel Pollack treats a spread as a relationship between images and positions. She also considers how cards support, constrain and develop one another. This guide draws on that approach and uses the ten positions currently shown in the DestinyPixel tarot tool. The grouping, questions and example below are our own teaching exercise.

## Check which Celtic Cross you are using

Books and tools use different position names and dealing orders. Here, the positions are Present situation, Crossing influence, Underlying pattern, Recent past, Conscious focus, Possible direction, Your approach, Surroundings, Hopes and concerns, and Potential outcome. Keep those definitions consistent throughout the reading.

Position five deserves attention. Here, “conscious focus” concerns what occupies your attention or what you consciously seek. Pollack calls the upper position in her version “possible outcome” and discusses its relationship to the final outcome. Those roles are not interchangeable. We are not treating our fifth position as an additional prediction card.

If spreads are new to you, start with the three-card worked example, then return when you want to handle more information.

## Do I need a separate significator before the ten cards?

A significator in the version compared here is deliberately selected before the deal to represent the person or subject of the question. Waite’s Part III, §7 places it face up, then shuffles the remaining pack and deals ten further cards. It is not an extra outcome discovered by that draw.

Pollack explicitly notes that not everyone uses a significator and that some readers use one only in particular spreads. She also points out a practical consequence of setting a card aside: it cannot appear elsewhere in that spread. These are observations in her own modern approach, not a universal requirement to choose or omit one. ## Start with facts you actually know

Imagine Lin wants to try running a weekend pottery class. A studio is available for one Saturday afternoon, and a friend with teaching experience has offered help. Several posts showing Lin's work received encouraging reactions, but nobody has confirmed a paid booking. The question is: “What should I do next to test whether this class is worth preparing further?”

The following cards are an invented teaching arrangement, all upright. This is neither a record of a real draw nor a case taken from Pollack's book.

| Position used here | First question | Hypothetical card |

| --- | --- | --- |

| 1 Present situation | What needs coordinating now? | Two of Pentacles |

| 2 Crossing influence | What brings support or constraint? | Three of Pentacles |

| 3 Underlying pattern | Which known motive or habit still matters? | Four of Pentacles |

| 4 Recent past | What recent experience affects the present judgment? | Eight of Wands |

| 5 Conscious focus | What am I concentrating on or seeking? | Seven of Cups |

| 6 Possible direction | What deserves observation if current conditions continue? | Page of Pentacles |

| 7 Your approach | How can I participate and respond? | King of Swords |

| 8 Surroundings | What do other people and conditions actually provide? | Three of Cups |

| 9 Hopes and concerns | Which expectation or worry needs clarification? | The Tower |

| 10 Potential outcome | What overall direction can we describe and then check? | Temperance |

## Read four relationships before ten separate speeches

These questions are learning prompts. Use the tool's own position labels, and consult the 78-card atlas when you need to check an image or a basic meaning.

Begin with positions one and two. The Two of Pentacles can prompt Lin to examine the coordination of time, materials and teaching. The Three of Pentacles brings collaboration into focus: what can the friend do, and does the studio permit a class? Cooperation may supply missing skills while adding coordination costs. Pollack explicitly allows the crossing card to support the first card. Whether this particular collaboration works requires an actual agreement.

Next, connect positions three, four and five. If Lin has already said that keeping costs manageable matters, the Four of Pentacles can bring that caution into the discussion. The Eight of Wands can connect with recent activity around sharing work. The Seven of Cups raises a question about the many classes Lin imagines offering: “Am I using encouraging feedback to justify developing too many options?” An “Underlying pattern” position does not give us permission to invent childhood experiences or hidden motives.

Then read six, seven and eight together. The Page of Pentacles can inspire a concrete learning experiment. The King of Swords suggests defining its terms: content, capacity and a booking deadline. The Three of Cups invites Lin to identify support from friends and community. Friends enjoying the pottery and people paying for a lesson remain separate facts to check. A short inquiry about interest is more useful than estimating bookings from a card.

Finally, connect nine and ten. In “hopes and concerns,” the Tower can help Lin clarify the worry: that the class may not happen, or that others may judge the public attempt? It cannot establish that something bad will happen to the studio. Temperance can bring the preceding conditions into a provisional plan: test a manageable class, adjust materials, timing and cooperation, then consider expansion. It does not guarantee market success.

These four groups are our exercise for this example. Pollack does not prescribe them as the single correct reading order.

## Read the crossing position and reversals separately

The second card lies sideways in the desktop Celtic Cross so that it crosses the first. That layout angle does not tell you whether its reading orientation is upright or reversed. Open the revealed card and read its displayed orientation. Decide whether to include reversals before shuffling; record that choice with your position definitions. A later change of method deserves a new note rather than a silent change to the original reading.

Waite describes the second position as the obstacle, including a favourable influence that does not help this particular question. Our tool uses the broader label “Crossing influence,” and the exercise above allows support as well as constraint. Keep that difference visible. Neither a position label nor a reversed card works like a minus sign: a reversed card in a challenging position does not automatically cancel the difficulty.

Reversal also has no single historical formula. In Part III, §3, Waite gives the reversed Sun the same general meaning at a lesser degree; his Seven of Cups entry moves from imagination without much substance toward intention and resolve. Those examples do not establish one rule for all cards. Modern lenses such as blocked expression, excess or an inward focus are interpretive choices; they are not Waite’s universal theory or evidence of a hidden cause.

For another invented variation on Lin’s class, change only position two to the Three of Pentacles reversed. Keep the question and the other nine cards as recorded above. As a modern RWS reading, try “the cooperation needs attention,” then distinguish two possibilities using information from the people involved:

| Possible reading | Information that would help distinguish it | A proportionate next step |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Responsibilities are unclear. | The friend is available, but each person expects the other to prepare materials. | Agree who prepares, teaches and welcomes participants, with dates. |

| The proposed help does not cover a practical need. | The friend can teach, but nobody has confirmed access to the studio or its equipment. | Ask the studio what is permitted and who handles access before announcing the class. |

## Practise one change, then return to the whole spread

The table offers two editorial hypotheses, not two facts discovered by the card. If roles and access are already confirmed, neither may fit. Write that down instead of inventing an uncooperative person. If both remain uncertain, ask the relevant questions; a second card is not a substitute for the studio’s answer.

Now reconnect this variation to positions seven and eight. The King of Swords can keep attention on clear terms; the Three of Cups can remind Lin to distinguish friendly encouragement from a specific commitment. The next step becomes “confirm the division of work and studio access,” rather than “cancel because collaboration will fail.” The exercise changes a planning question, not a guaranteed future.

Try it on paper: preserve the all-upright example, mark the single changed orientation in a separate copy, and write what changes in the interpretation, what remains supported, and what new information you need. Do not keep redrawing to make the original answer disappear. If you use the digital table, practise with the cards actually drawn; this written arrangement is not a preset you can request.

This exercise uses Rider–Waite–Smith imagery and the position definitions on this page. For another deck or Celtic Cross variant, check its guide and labels before carrying the interpretation across. Record which source or modern lens you chose, so that a later review can distinguish a method change from new information.

## Finish with something you can check

Lin's note might read: “The space and offer of help deserve confirmation. Positive reactions have not established paid demand. I will share a trial-class invitation with clear content, cost and timing, observe the actual response, and adjust its scale.”

Every card has contributed without taking over the conclusion. If the reading remains unclear, identify the missing fact, uncertain position definition or unresolved relationship first. The three-card connection guide offers practice with linking language and alternative interpretations. We use the spread to organize reflection, without presenting it as a scientifically validated prediction procedure.
