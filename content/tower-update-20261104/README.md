## What the reader gets

The existing article already close-reads the image, records both Waite passages, says to handle the impact before the meaning, compares nearby cards, and keeps both cases, the practice and the misreading note. It did not say how Rachel Pollack reads the yellow drops, why the Tower follows the Devil and Temperance, or how she connects Waite’s reversed “imprisonment” with a milder shock. Those three points are now in the Chinese article once each.

1. After the last paragraph of「画面把稳固与失重摆在一起」, with no new heading: the drops as the Hebrew letter yod, read as grace rather than anger or punishment; her count of twenty-two, ten on the left and twelve on the right, matching the major arcana. Marked as her symbolic count, not Waite’s text and not a universal rule. Practical use: do not read the disruption as the querent’s karmic punishment. The twenty-two count stays because the Commons RWS scan used by this article shows ten drops on the left and twelve on the right.
2. After「把相近的牌义分清楚」, a new subsection titled exactly「为什么高塔排在恶魔与节制后面」(`depth-sequence`): Devil, then Temperance, then Tower; Jung’s dam, sluice and blasted dam; external blows often arrive with unprocessed issues but cannot all be blamed on the querent; one practical post-check question; Devil nearby and Star after; Pollack says the meaning needs the reader’s experience and the other cards (printed/PDF 157).
3. After the reversed paragraph of「先处理影响，再讨论意义」, with no new heading: Waite’s reversed “imprisonment” as a third reading via Pollack 157. The shock is milder, and holding the reaction in keeps the process from finishing, as if locked in the tower. Require evidence, such as the same issue returning, and do not default to “repression”.

No Babel story, no 16-reduced-to-7 Chariot correspondence, and no new case or myth was added.
